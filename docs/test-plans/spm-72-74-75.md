# SPM-72, SPM-74 and SPM-76: choosing a venue for an event request

Acceptance criteria and test cases for SPM-72 (venues in the event request,
coordinator), SPM-74 (View Venue Availability) and SPM-76 (Filtered Venue
Search, organiser), in each ticket's Jira format, ready to paste. Implemented on
backend and frontend `feature/SPM-72-74-76-coordinators-venue`; Master 1.5,
decisions T29 and T30.

Workflow (developer brief of 23 October 2026): the organiser browses the
venues while creating the request and ranks their choices (SPM-76). The
assigned coordinator reviews the request: the Venue section opens on the
organiser's choices, and the search, filters, comparison, availability calendar
and venue list open only when the coordinator looks for another venue (SPM-72,
SPM-74). A coordinator's selection is a suggestion to the organiser. What
follows (the organiser accepting it, the booking request to Venue Staff and
their decision) belongs to SPM-179, SPM-186 and SPM-189.

## SPM-72 — Venues in the Event Request

Changes from the Jira text of 23 October 2026:

* AC1: the section opens on the organiser's choices; the search is behind "Find another venue".
* AC3: "Show 10, 20, 50 or 100 venues" and "Show more venues (N more)".
* AC7: the toggle is named "Show comparison view".
* AC8: the change history reads "Venue suggested" with the venue name.
* TC-VC-001, 004, 006, 007, 008 and 014: updated to match.

As an Event Coordinator, I want to browse, compare and select venues inside each event request so that I can choose the venue that best fits the event's requirements.

ACs:

* AC1 — Access: the assigned Event Coordinator browses venues in the Venue section of the event request page. The section opens on the organiser's venue choices in their order (a choice that is no longer available says so); the search, filters, comparison, availability calendar and venue list stay collapsed until the coordinator selects Find another venue. A coordinator who is not assigned to the event is told the event is not found.
* AC2 — Coordinator Lead: the Lead who supervises the event (made its current assignment) can open the event read-only and search, filter, sort, view and compare its venues, but cannot select a venue. The Lead does not review the coordinator's selection.
* AC3 — Search, filter and sort: the coordinator can search by venue name or location; filter by minimum capacity, layout, facility and accessibility; enter required facilities; and sort by best match, name, capacity, setup time or turnaround time, ascending or descending. The list shows 10 venues by default; the coordinator can choose to show 10, 20, 50 or 100 at a time, and Show more venues adds the same number again. When no venue matches, the system says so and offers to clear the filters. Invalid values (e.g. capacity 0) show an error against the field.
* AC4 — Venue details (read-only): each venue shows its photos, name, address, capacity, operating hours, setup time, turnaround time, rooms, supported layouts, facilities, accessibility features, emergency access, known restrictions and description. Nothing can be edited. Only venues that are active and in service are shown; deactivated, inactive and under-maintenance venues are never listed or selectable.
* AC5 — Ranking and highlighting: venues meeting every requirement of the request (capacity for the expected attendance, availability on the event date, preferred layout, each accessibility need and each required facility) are listed first, followed by venues with the fewest unmet requirements. Requirements that are met are highlighted in light green; unmet requirements are shown in light grey.
* AC6 — Availability for the event date: each venue shows whether it is available for the event's date and time, including its setup and turnaround time, and lists any booking, hold or block that overlaps. An operating-hours issue is a warning, not a block. The event's own bookings and holds never make a venue unavailable. When availability cannot be checked, the venue is shown as "Error checking availability", never as available.
* AC7 — Comparison: a Show comparison view button opens a second panel so two venues can be compared side by side. The second panel is hidden by default and can be closed again.
* AC8 — Selection: while the event is Under Review or in Planning, the coordinator can select one venue, from the organiser's choices or the venue list, after confirming. The selection is saved as the coordinator's suggested venue, recorded in the event's change history as "Venue suggested" with the venue's name (and the suggestion it replaces) and sent to the Organiser as a notification. It does not hold or book the venue. A venue that cannot hold the expected attendance cannot be selected. Selecting another venue replaces the suggestion.
* AC9 — Organiser view: the Organiser sees the suggested venue (name, address, capacity, who suggested it and when) on their event request, stated as not yet booked.
* AC10 — Integrity: a double-click or resubmission records one suggestion and sends one notification. If the suggestion was changed elsewhere, or the venue stopped being available, the system explains why, changes nothing and offers a reload. A failed save keeps the page as it was.
* AC11 — Empty and failure states: if no venues are available, the system informs the coordinator. If venue details cannot be loaded, layout, facility and accessibility requirements show as "Error loading details". If the venue list cannot be loaded, an error with a retry is shown.

## SPM-74 — View Venue Availability

Changes from the text of 23 October 2026:

* The coordinator's calendar toggle sits directly above the calendar.
* Unchecked availability reads "Error checking availability".
* TC16 and TC17 are updated to match.

As an Event Coordinator or Venue Staff, I want to view a venue availability calendar across dates and times, including confirmed bookings, active holds, setup and turnaround buffers, and operational blocks, so that I can accurately determine when a venue can be requested without creating scheduling conflicts.

ACs:

* Only authenticated Event Coordinators and Venue Staff can access venue availability calendars.

    * Event Coordinators view availability inside the event request's Venue section, after selecting Find another venue: the Show availability calendar button sits directly above the calendar, which opens for the venues being viewed or compared, on the event's date, with the event's time already checked. There is no separate availability page for Event Coordinators.
    * Venue Staff view availability for venue management and booking decisions at Venues > Availability.
    * Event Coordinator Leads and other roles receive an access-denied response.

* Each venue in the event's Venue section shows its availability status for the event's date and time, including setup and turnaround time; the event's own bookings and holds never make it unavailable.
* The calendar displays venue availability for a selected date range and time period.
* Each calendar entry identifies:

    * Venue name
    * Date and time
    * Availability status
    * Entry type, such as confirmed booking, active hold, or operational block
    * Related event or booking reference where applicable

* Confirmed or approved bookings are displayed as unavailable periods.
* The calendar must prevent the same venue from appearing available when another active booking overlaps the requested occupied period.
* Active tentative holds are displayed as unavailable and must include their expiry date and time.
* Expired holds must no longer block the venue or appear as active availability restrictions.
* Operational blocks created by Venue Staff are displayed as unavailable periods.
* The calendar identifies the block reason, such as:

    * Maintenance
    * Equipment failure
    * Renovation
    * Safety
    * Other

* The block note is visible to authorised users.
* The system identifies overlapping occupied periods between bookings, holds, and operational blocks.

    * Adjacent periods are allowed.
    * Any overlap, including an overlap of one second, is treated as a conflict.

* The system clearly distinguishes between:

    * Available
    * Unavailable due to confirmed booking
    * Unavailable due to active hold
    * Unavailable due to operational block
    * Potentially suitable with an operating-hours warning
    * Error checking availability (the availability data could not be read)

* A venue cannot be shown as available if its occupied period conflicts with an active booking, hold, or operational block.
* If any part of the occupied period falls outside the venue's operating hours, the calendar displays a warning.

    Operating-hours violations are warnings rather than automatic booking blocks.

* The calendar reflects the latest committed booking, hold, block, and cancellation changes after refresh or reload.

    The system must not show a venue as available before a successful backend update is completed.

* The calendar provides appropriate feedback when:

    * Data is loading
    * No venues or availability entries exist
    * The requested date range has no records
    * The availability request fails
    * The user does not have permission to view the calendar

## SPM-76 — Filtered Venue Search

As an Event Organiser, I want to browse venues with basic filtering so that I can understand what venues might suit my event.

ACs:

* AC1 — Access: while creating an event request or editing a draft, the Event Organiser browses the available venues in the Venue & Layout Requirements step. Only venues that are active and in service are listed. Other roles cannot use the organiser's venue list.
* AC2 — Search, filter, sort and page size: the organiser can search by venue name or location; filter by minimum capacity, layout, facility and accessibility; enter required facilities; and sort by best match, name, capacity, setup time or turnaround time, ascending or descending. The list shows 10 venues by default; the organiser can choose to show 10, 20, 50 or 100 at a time, and Show more venues adds the same number again. When no venue matches, the system says so and offers to clear the filters. Invalid values show an error against the field. Pressing Enter in a filter applies it and never submits the request.
* AC3 — Requirements and availability: each venue is checked against what the request states so far: capacity for the expected attendance, availability on the proposed date and time including the venue's setup and turnaround time, the preferred layout, each accessibility need and each required facility. Venues meeting every requirement come first; met requirements are light green and unmet ones light grey. Each venue shows Available, Unavailable (booking, hold or block) or an operating-hours warning, but never which events or bookings make it unavailable. When availability cannot be checked, the venue shows "Error checking availability", never available.
* AC4 — Venue details (read-only): each venue shows its photos, name, address, capacity, operating hours, setup time, turnaround time, rooms, supported layouts, facilities, accessibility features, emergency access, known restrictions and description. Nothing can be edited.
* AC5 — Comparison: a Show comparison view button opens a second panel so two venues can be compared side by side. The second panel is hidden by default and can be closed again.
* AC6 — Choosing venues: the organiser chooses an ideal venue and, optionally, alternatives, up to 20 in order of preference, and can reorder and remove them. The choices are saved with the draft and submitted with the request as its venue preferences; choosing does not hold or book a venue. The coordinator reviewing the request sees these choices first (SPM-72 AC1).
* AC7 — Empty and failure states: if no venues are available, the system says so. If the venue list cannot be loaded, an error with a retry is shown and the organiser can still continue the request without choosing a venue. If venue details cannot be loaded, layout, facility and accessibility requirements show "Error loading details".
