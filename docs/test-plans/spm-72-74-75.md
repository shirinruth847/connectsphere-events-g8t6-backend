# SPM-72, SPM-74 and SPM-75: choosing a venue for an event request

Acceptance criteria and test cases for SPM-72 (venues in the event request),
SPM-74 (View Venue Availability) and SPM-75 (Search for a Venue), in each
ticket's Jira format. SPM-75 below reflects Jira as read on 26 October 2026
and Master 1.6 (T31); the SPM-72/74 sections retain their documented scope
changes from the earlier implementation. Current branches: backend
`feature/SPM-72-75-related-venues`, frontend
`feature/SPM-72-75-venue-popup-related-venues`. This document is a test plan,
not a claim that every listed hosted check has been executed.

Workflow (developer brief of 23 October 2026): the assigned coordinator
reviews the request. The Venue section opens on the organiser's ranked venue
preferences; the search (SPM-75), filters, comparison, availability calendar
(SPM-74) and venue list open only when the coordinator looks for another
venue. A coordinator's selection is a suggestion to the organiser. What
follows (the organiser accepting it, the booking request to Venue Staff and
their decision) belongs to SPM-179, SPM-186 and SPM-189. Organiser venue
browsing (SPM-76) is kept on `feature/SPM-76-organiser-venue-search` for later.

## SPM-72 — Venues in the Event Request

Changes from the Jira text of 23 October 2026:

* AC1: the section opens on the organiser's choices; the search is behind "Find another venue".
* AC3: "Show 1–100 venues" (12 initially), "Show more venues (N more)" and the list window of two rows of three cards; the search itself is SPM-75.
* AC6: a multi-day event is checked on every day.
* AC7: the toggle is named "Show comparison view".
* AC8: the change history reads "Venue suggested" with the venue name.
* TC-VC-001, 004, 006, 007, 008 and 014: updated to match.

As an Event Coordinator, I want to browse, compare and select venues inside each event request so that I can choose the venue that best fits the event's requirements.

ACs:

* AC1 — Access: the assigned Event Coordinator browses venues in the Venue section of the event request page. The section opens on the organiser's venue choices in their order (a choice that is no longer available says so); the search, filters, comparison, availability calendar and venue list stay collapsed until the coordinator selects Find another venue. A coordinator who is not assigned to the event is told the event is not found.
* AC2 — Coordinator Lead: the Lead who supervises the event (made its current assignment) can open the event read-only and search, filter, sort, view and compare its venues, but cannot select a venue. The Lead does not review the coordinator's selection.
* AC3 — Search, filter and sort: the coordinator can search by venue name or location; filter by minimum capacity, layout, facility and accessibility; enter required facilities; and sort by best match, name, capacity, setup time or turnaround time, ascending or descending. The list shows 12 venues by default; the coordinator can enter a whole number from 1 to 100 at a time, and Show more venues adds the same number again. The venue cards sit in a fixed window two rows of three cards tall that scrolls, so the page stays short. When no venue matches, the system says so and offers to clear the filters. Invalid values (e.g. capacity 0) show an error against the field.
* AC4 — Venue details (read-only): each venue shows its photos, name, address, capacity, operating hours, setup time, turnaround time, rooms, supported layouts, facilities, accessibility features, emergency access, known restrictions and description. Nothing can be edited. Only venues that are active and in service are shown; deactivated, inactive and under-maintenance venues are never listed or selectable.
* AC5 — Ranking and highlighting: venues meeting every requirement of the request (capacity for the expected attendance, availability on the event date, preferred layout, each accessibility need and each required facility) are listed first, followed by venues with the fewest unmet requirements. Requirements that are met are highlighted in light green; unmet requirements are shown in light grey.
* AC6 — Availability for the event date: each venue shows whether it is available for the event's date and time, including its setup and turnaround time; a multi-day event occupies the venue from its first day's start to its last day's end, so a booking, hold or block on any of its days makes the venue unavailable; and lists any booking, hold or block that overlaps. An operating-hours issue is a warning, not a block. The event's own bookings and holds never make a venue unavailable. When availability cannot be checked, the venue is shown as "Error checking availability", never as available.
* AC7 — Comparison: a Show comparison view button opens a second panel so two venues can be compared side by side. The second panel is hidden by default and can be closed again.
* AC8 — Selection: while the event is Under Review or in Planning, the coordinator can select one venue, from the organiser's choices or the venue list, after confirming. The selection is saved as the coordinator's suggested venue, recorded in the event's change history as "Venue suggested" with the venue's name (and the suggestion it replaces) and sent to the Organiser as a notification. It does not hold or book the venue. A venue that cannot hold the expected attendance cannot be selected. Selecting another venue replaces the suggestion.
* AC9 — Organiser view: the Organiser sees the suggested venue (name, address, capacity, who suggested it and when) on their event request, stated as not yet booked.
* AC10 — Integrity: a double-click or resubmission records one suggestion and sends one notification. If the suggestion was changed elsewhere, or the venue stopped being available, the system explains why, changes nothing and offers a reload. A failed save keeps the page as it was.
* AC11 — Empty and failure states: if no venues are available, the system informs the coordinator. If venue details cannot be loaded, layout, facility and accessibility requirements show as "Error loading details". If the venue list cannot be loaded, an error with a retry is shown.

## SPM-74 — View Venue Availability

Changes from the text of 23 October 2026:

* The coordinator's calendar toggle sits directly above the calendar.
* A multi-day event is checked from its first start to its last end; the calendar's period gains an end date.
* Unchecked availability reads "Error checking availability".
* TC16 and TC17 are updated to match.

As an Event Coordinator or Venue Staff, I want to view a venue availability calendar across dates and times, including confirmed bookings, active holds, setup and turnaround buffers, and operational blocks, so that I can accurately determine when a venue can be requested without creating scheduling conflicts.

ACs:

* Only authenticated Event Coordinators and Venue Staff can access venue availability calendars.

    * Event Coordinators view availability inside the event request's Venue section, after selecting Find another venue: the Show availability calendar button sits directly above the calendar, which opens for the venues being viewed or compared, on the event's date, with the event's time already checked. There is no separate availability page for Event Coordinators.
    * Venue Staff view availability for venue management and booking decisions at Venues > Availability.
    * Event Coordinator Leads and other roles receive an access-denied response.

* Each venue in the event's Venue section shows its availability status for the event's date and time, including setup and turnaround time; the event's own bookings and holds never make it unavailable.
* A multi-day event occupies the venue as one block from its first day's start (less setup) to its last day's end (plus turnaround); a booking, hold or block on any of its days makes the venue unavailable. Nights outside operating hours are a warning, not a block.
* The requested period to check has a start date and time and an end date and time, so it can span several days.
* The calendar displays venue availability for a selected date range and time period.
* Each calendar entry identifies:

    * Venue name
    * Date and time
    * Availability status
    * Entry type, such as confirmed booking, active hold, or operational block
    * Related event or booking reference where applicable

* Confirmed or approved bookings are displayed as unavailable periods.
* The calendar must prevent the same venue from appearing available when another active booking overlaps the requested occupied period.
* Active tentative holds are displayed as unavailable and must include their expiry date and time.
* Expired holds must no longer block the venue or appear as active availability restrictions.
* Operational blocks created by Venue Staff are displayed as unavailable periods.
* The calendar identifies the block reason, such as:

    * Maintenance
    * Equipment failure
    * Renovation
    * Safety
    * Other

* The block note is visible to authorised users.
* The system identifies overlapping occupied periods between bookings, holds, and operational blocks.

    * Adjacent periods are allowed.
    * Any overlap, including an overlap of one second, is treated as a conflict.

* The system clearly distinguishes between:

    * Available
    * Unavailable due to confirmed booking
    * Unavailable due to active hold
    * Unavailable due to operational block
    * Potentially suitable with an operating-hours warning
    * Error checking availability (the availability data could not be read)

* A venue cannot be shown as available if its occupied period conflicts with an active booking, hold, or operational block.
* If any part of the occupied period falls outside the venue's operating hours, the calendar displays a warning.

    Operating-hours violations are warnings rather than automatic booking blocks.

* The calendar reflects the latest committed booking, hold, block, and cancellation changes after refresh or reload.

    The system must not show a venue as available before a successful backend update is completed.

* The calendar provides appropriate feedback when:

    * Data is loading
    * No venues or availability entries exist
    * The requested date range has no records
    * The availability request fails
    * The user does not have permission to view the calendar

## SPM-75 — Search for a Venue

Current Jira baseline: 26 October 2026; Master 1.6, T31. The API retains
`requiredFacilities` for compatibility and requirement ranking, but the
coordinator's search has no Rank by facilities control.

ACs:

* AC1 — Access: the assigned coordinator opens Find another venue from the event request in a popup, closed by its close button or Escape. The supervising Lead can search read-only; users outside the event cannot load its venues.
* AC2 — Search box: search by venue name/location and minimum capacity share one line. Apply filters or Enter applies; Clear filters resets. Capacity is a whole number from 1 to the system maximum. Invalid capacity or a search over 150 characters shows a field error without changing the results.
* AC3 — Multi-select filters: Layout, Facility and Accessibility are checkbox dropdowns, initially showing their names as grey placeholders. They list venue values and keep ticked values after narrowing. Each permits at most 10 distinct values. Escape/click outside closes only the dropdown. There is no Rank by facilities filter.
* AC4 — Matching: values within a filter match any selected value; all filters used must match. Matching ignores case.
* AC5 — Available venues: only active, in-service venues are listed; deactivated, inactive and under-maintenance venues are excluded.
* AC6 — Results and sorting: show the matching/available count and the count meeting every requirement. Support best match, name, capacity, setup and turnaround sorts in the chosen direction. Show three cards per row on a laptop.
* AC7 — Number shown: show 12 venues initially. Show accepts whole numbers from 1 to 100; Show more adds the last valid count or the remainder. Invalid input leaves results unchanged and restores the last valid number on blur. The list scrolls inside its own window, about two card rows tall.
* AC8 — Empty/failure states: distinguish no matches (with Clear filters) from no available venues. Unreadable detail data shows Error loading venue details and reports the Facility filter as not checked; layout/accessibility fall back to basic records. Availability remains unknown, never available. List failure offers Retry.

**Test cases:**

* TC-VS-001 — Open the event-scoped popup, search Hall with capacity 200, apply, and clear. Check the search/capacity line, matching venue names/addresses, and restored results.
* TC-VS-002 — Submit capacity 0 and a 151-character search. Each shows a field error and preserves the prior list.
* TC-VS-003 — Select Theatre and Banquet layouts, then Projector and Stage facilities. Either value within one filter can match; Banquet plus Stage must match both filters. Check case-insensitive matching and accessibility filtering.
* TC-VS-004 — Check placeholder labels, selected-value text, dropdown-only Escape handling, persistence of selected choices after narrowing, and rejection of more than 10 values.
* TC-VS-005 — With 14 venues at 1440×1000, verify three cards per row, a two-row scrolling window and Show more venues (2 more).
* TC-VS-006 — With 23 venues, verify 12 initially and 11 more; then all 23. Enter 7, then 0 and blur: seven remain and the box returns to 7. Enter 50: show all 23 and no more button.
* TC-VS-007 — Sort different capacities ascending and descending; check the count line and the documented requirements-first grouping.
* TC-VS-008 — Exclude under-maintenance venues; check list failure/Retry. With details unavailable and a Facility filter selected, report it as not checked and never claim availability.
* TC-VS-009 — Reject an unassigned coordinator and other roles. Allow only the assigned coordinator and the supervising Lead, read-only for the Lead.

Backend regression cases also verify that Fire alarm system does not meet
PA system, and Hearing protection does not meet Hearing loop. Complete
labels match after case/punctuation normalization; documented wheelchair
label aliases preserve the existing equivalent-label behavior.
